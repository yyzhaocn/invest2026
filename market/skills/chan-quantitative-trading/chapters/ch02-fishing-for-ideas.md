# Chapter 2: Fishing for Ideas

## Core Idea
Choose a strategy that fits your capital, time, skills and goal — then vet it with six hard questions before trusting it.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Identify a Strategy that Suits You
- **Working hours**: intraday needs full attention; daily/weekly fits a day job.
- **Programming skills**: match strategy complexity to your code ability.
- **Trading capital**: low capital → high-leverage-friendly strategies (futures/options); high capital → diversified, lower leverage.
- **Goal**: information ratio target, max drawdown tolerance.

### The 6 Questions (vet any idea)
1. **Benchmark & consistency**: beats SPY/benchmark? Edge consistent, not one lucky year?
2. **Drawdown depth & length**: worst case deep? long? Can you survive it?
3. **Transaction costs**: profit *after* realistic costs? (costs are the #1 killer)
4. **Survivorship bias**: does your data include dead stocks? Biased data inflates results.
5. **Performance over the years**: does the edge persist across regimes (bull/bear/crash)?
6. **Data-snooping bias**: many variations tried, only best reported → live Sharpe will degrade.
- **Bonus: "fly under the radar"** — is the strategy still uncrowded (institutions haven't arbitraged it away)?

### Plausible Strategy Sources
- Academic papers, trader forums, Twitter, machine-learning communities — with a critical eye; published ideas are often broken or crowded (example: Wealth-Lab strategy that only worked after the author's variations).

## Key Concepts
- **Information ratio**: annualized Sharpe (the Chan-standard headline metric).
- **Survivorship bias**: database missing delisted/bankrupt names.
- **Data-snooping bias**: overfitting via too many trials.
- **Crowding**: institutional adoption erodes edge.

## Key Takeaways
1. Fit the strategy to your constraints first (capital/time/skill), then to the market.
2. Run every idea through the 6 questions — a "yes to all" is rare and valuable.
3. Expect published strategies to need refinement; variations (holding period, timing) are where real edges hide.
4. Costs and survivorship bias are the two silent backtest killers.
