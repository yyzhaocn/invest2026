# Chapter 1: The Whats, Whos, and Whys of Quantitative Trading

## Core Idea
Quantitative trading is a systematic, scalable business — almost anyone with programming skill, patience and discipline can build one; the key advantages are scalability and low time demand, not genius.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Who Can Become a Quantitative Trader?
- Requires: programming skills, interest in finance, statistical intuition, capital.
- Not restricted to PhDs — disciplined systematic process beats raw IQ.

### The Business Case (3 advantages)
- **Scalability**: rules-based strategies scale across markets and capital without your time scaling linearly.
- **Demand on time**: once automated, little ongoing time; the process (research → backtest → deploy) is the job.
- **Nonnecessity of marketing**: no clients to sell to (personal trading); strategy quality is the only currency.

### The Way Forward
- Process: idea → backtest → paper trade → deploy → monitor/refine.

## Key Concepts
- **Quantitative trading**: systematic, rules-based trading with statistical edge.
- **Algorithmic trading business**: treated as a business — process, risk, capital discipline.
- **Scalability**: edge transfers across instruments/sizes.

## Key Takeaways
1. Quant trading is a business process, not a hobby — treat capital as business capital.
2. Automation buys back your time; the research loop is where the value is.
3. Your edge must be statistical and rules-based (else it's not "quantitative").
