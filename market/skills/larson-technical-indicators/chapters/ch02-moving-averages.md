# Ch2 — Moving Averages

**Theme**: The workhorse trend indicator — what the average represents, which to use, how to confirm.

## Core Ideas
- A moving average = average closing price over a period; its **total represents the stock's price over that timeframe** (not just a math result).
- **30-day MA** is commonly used for short-term buy/sell. Above = upside potential; below = odds of moving lower → **place a stop-loss**.
- **200-day MA** = the **strongest support/resistance** for stocks and indexes (surpassed 10/20-day popularity after the 1990s).
- **Two types**: Simple (cleaner, slower signal) vs Exponential (faster, noisier). **Larson mostly uses SMA** (except two daily indicators where EMA works best).
- **Speed**: shorter MA = faster movement; longer = slower.
- **10 & 20-day**: never go long/short unless these align. 10 crosses below 20 = short; 10 crosses above 20 = strength/long.
- **Confirm over ≥3 consecutive days** before acting on a crossover.

## Timely Adjustment
- In a **bullish position when the market may change course**, **shorten** your moving average → quicker sell signal (don't keep 30/40-day while trend turns).

## Volume companion (from Ch1 carried through)
- Watch volume bars rise above the 20-day average alongside MA moves — earlier, better entries/exits ("timing is the most important part").

## Self-test anchors
- Strongest MA = 200-day. Fastest = 3-day.
- Buy confirmed **3 days after** the faster MA crosses above the slower one.
- Volume = law of supply/demand; further confirmation of a crossover = volume + 3 days without reversing.
- Bullish position → **shorten** the MA period.
