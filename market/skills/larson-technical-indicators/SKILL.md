# 12 Simple Technical Indicators That Really Work
**Author**: Mark Larson | **Year**: 2007 | **Format**: Course Book (print/DVD) | **Core idea**: Technical indicators matter more than fundamentals — but *parameters* decide everything.

## Core Mental Models & Operating Principles

### 1. Parameters Are Everything (Ch1)
The single most essential aspect of any indicator is **its parameters**. The right parameter gives better entry/exit points. Do not use default settings blindly — tune per indicator and per market regime. Larson back-tests every approach ≥6 months before risking real money.

### 2. Technicals > Fundamentals, But Only for *When* (Ch1)
Technical indicators tell you **which stocks to buy/sell and WHEN**, which fundamentals cannot. Fundamentals for selection, technicals for timing.

### 3. Volume Goes With the Trend (Ch1) — the law of supply/demand
- **Normal**: volume rises as price rises, falls as price falls (healthy trend).
- **Downside breakout + expanding volume** = very bearish (more sellers, more enthusiasm to sell).
- **Upside breakout MUST expand volume** to confirm enthusiasm (favor moves to demand/buyers).
- **Larson's trigger**: daily volume ≥ **1.5× the average volume over the past 20 days** = a sign of commitment, raises odds the stock will move (the more volume, the bigger the move).

### 4. Change of Character (Ch3/4) — the buy/sell pivot
A stock "changes character" when it transitions **bearish → bullish** (accumulation → buy) or **bullish → bearish** (distribution → sell). Look for this **across multiple indicator tabs**. That's the signal to act.

### 5. The 12-Plus/Tab System (Ch3, Ch10)
Track the SAME stock across multiple indicator "tabs" (MA, volume, BOP, MACD, PRC, TSV, RSI, Inertia, ATR…). Tally how many give a **positive** vs **negative** read. Act when more point one direction. **Stock scans** automate this across hundreds of stocks — filters = indicators + parameters that summarize a stock's behavior.

### 6. Confirmation Before Acting (Ch2/5)
Never trust a single bar or a single indicator. **Confirm a moving-average signal over ≥3 consecutive days**. For PRC, wait for both the 21-day and 15-day to confirm (drop below / rise above) before acting.

### 7. Two Indicator Styles (Ch1)
- **Lagging indicators** — move after price (moving averages).
- **Leading indicators** — change before price (TSV, money stream, BOP; also MACD/PRC are treated as leading).
- Leading indicators (price+volume based) are where the edge is.

## The Indicators (with Larson's exact parameters & rules)

### Moving Averages (Ch2)
- **Definition**: average of closing prices over a period; the total represents the stock's average price over that window.
- **Short-term buy/sell**: **30-day MA** is commonly used. Price above 30-day → greater upside potential; below → greater odds of moving lower.
- **STOP LOSS habit**: once price drops below the 30-day MA, place a stop-loss order (see ATR).
- **Strongest MA**: the **200-day MA** = the strongest point of support/resistance for stocks and indexes.
- **SMA vs EMA**: Simple = slower/cleaner signals; Exponential = faster, noisier signals. **Larson almost always uses SMA**, except for the two daily indicators where EMA works best.
- **Speed**: shorter MA = faster movement; longer = slower.
- **Confirm MA crossover over ≥3 consecutive days**.
- **10 & 20-day**: never go long/short unless these align; 10-day crossing THROUGH the 20-day = short (down) or strength/long (up) trigger.
- **Adjusting**: in a bullish position when market may turn, **SHORTEN your moving average** → quicker sell signal (don't keep a 30/40-day while trend is turning).

### Balance of Power — BOP (Ch3)
- **What**: tells whether underlying action is systematic **buying accumulation** or **distribution**. Plotted as accumulation above/below a zero (center/level) line. Measures institutional buying/selling (blocks of ≥10,000 shares).
- **Read**: more buying → stock up; more selling → stock down; lack of either → neutral/sideways until it intensifies.
- **Larson's parameter**: **12-day SMA** on BOP works best — 9/10 enter too early, 14/15 miss the low. Don't mind missing; better early discipline.
- **Signal**: wait for BOP to confirm + a **moving-average cross of the zero/center line** before entering; then ride (e.g., in at $10, out at $14).

### MACD (Ch4) — 90% success paired with PRC
- **90% win rate**: when Larson identifies the *exact crossing* of MACD & PRC, he wins ~90% of the time.
- **Structure**: uses **three EMA's** (not 1-2). Adjust any parameter — the right number is key.
- **Signal**: powerful when lines **converge/touch** (not necessarily cross) and price confirms. Look for patterns where the three moving averages come together closely (≈ within touching; ~eighth-of-an-inch close). If too far apart, the setup doesn't work.
- **Preferred view**: **histogram form** — makes buy/sell "change of character" clear (bull→bear = sell, bear→bull = buy).

### Price Rate of Change — PRC (Ch5) — 90% success paired with MACD
- **Parameter (the "secret ingredient")**: **21-day** — divide current close by the close **21 days ago**. Companion line: **15-day PRC**.
- **50% level is the line in the sand**: PRC above 50% = bullish; **crosses below 50% = bearish**.
- **Selling rule**: if using 21-day (top) + 15-day (bottom) and PRC drops below → **seriously sell 100%** (conservative: at least 50%). Ideally sell ½ immediately, ½ when it drops below the level.
- **Managthem**: wait 4 more trading days → likely gap down; confirm both 21-day AND 15-day break below before selling. Buy when 21-day PRC goes above → positive signal (wait 3 consecutive days confirmation).

### Time-Segmented Volume — TSV (Ch6/7) — leading, volume-based
- A **leading indicator** based on price + volume. (Companion usage: RSI + TSV in Ch7 for confirmation.)

### RSI (Ch7)
- Relative Strength Index — used together with TSV. (Larson frames RSI+TSV as confirming each other rather than standalone.)

### Inertia (Ch8)
- **Scale 0-100**. **Negative inertia = bullish; positive inertia = bearish** (Larson's convention).
- **Use**: price must trend in the SAME direction as inertia for confirmation. Inertia turning **below zero** (negative) = **positive setup / long-term uptrend confirmation**.
- Powerful but **should not be used alone** — combine with other indicators.
- Exact 0-100 setting shown on indicator chart; confirms "change of character" (e.g., Inertia moved negative→positive-confidence).

### ATR — Average True Range (Ch9) — stops & option exits
- **Use**: set **trailing/volatility-based stop-loss**; the strongest for protecting profits & limiting losses.
- **Optimization on a downtrend**: when price falls below 30-day MA, place a stop at a selected price.
- **Option selling exit**: sell the option based on what **the stock does** (ATR-based stop), NOT on a % of the option cost — most option traders sell for a loss on cost; Larson exits on underlying move.

### Stock Scans — the 6-Indicator Scan (Ch10)
- Combine ≥6 technical ingredients (indicators+parameters+timeframes) as a filter to find "cream of the crop" instead of scanning hundreds by hand.
- Bullish scan example: price above 3 moving averages + volume/momentum confirmations. Bearish scans mirror it.
- Scans eliminate tedium and let you track strong candidates in all market conditions. Iterate to improve precision over time.

## Three Investment Strategies That Work in Any Market (Ch11)

1. **Buy stocks that are going up / "make the trend your friend"** — momentum alignment (buy when price & volume confirm uptrend). Options: buy call / covered variants aligned with the trend.
2. **Multi-indicator confirmation** — act only when multiple indicator tabs give buy signals together.
3. **Adapt parameters to the regime** — change your indicator parameters when the market changes from bullish to bearish (or vice-versa). Guard: you can go long in a bear market, but never short a bull market.

## Tells & Anti-Patterns

- **Buy-the-dip on a weakly-trending stock** (the Intel $100→$86→$65 example): just because a stock is "cheaper than you paid" doesn't make it a buy — if technicals say it's weak, it keeps dropping. **Use a stop loss.**
- **Ignoring parameters** — default indicator settings waste the method; tuning is the edge.
- **Trusting a single bar/indicator** — always confirm (3 days, or 21+15-day PRC, or BOP+MA-cross).
- **Gap conviction**: a gap DOWN is more significant than a gap up for bearish signals.
- **Shorting a bull market** — against the strategy; never short into strength.

## Canonical Numbers to Remember
- **1.5×** = volume trigger (vs 20-day avg) signal of commitment
- **30-day MA** = short-term buy/sell; drop below → set stop
- **200-day MA** = strongest support/resistance
- **3-day** = MA/PRC confirmation window
- **BOP 12-day SMA** = optimal BOP parameter
- **PRC 21 + 15 day**; **50% level** = bullish/bearish line
- **MACD/PRC** = 90% success at exact crossover
- **Inertia 0-100**, negative = bullish (Larson convention)

*(Technical indicators are for risk management and research reference, not a guarantee of profit.)*
