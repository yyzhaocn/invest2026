# Chapter 11: The Trader's Mind

## Core Idea
Emotions chemically unbalance decision-making; cognitive biases distort every judgment — the only defense is pre-committed rules and honest journaling.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Emotion & Stress
- Stress/emotion **unbalance the brain chemically** → overweighting recent losses, overtrading, revenge trading.
- Euphoria and fear both drive the same failure: abandoning the plan.

### Cognitive Biases (trading-specific)
- **Confirmation bias**: seeking evidence for your position.
- **Hindsight bias**: "it was obvious" after the fact (erodes learning).
- **Loss aversion**: cutting winners, holding losers.
- **Recency**: extrapolating the last few trades.
- **Anchoring**: stuck on entry price when managing.

### The Defense
- Rules committed in writing BEFORE the trade; journaling after; process-based (not outcome-based) evaluation.

## Key Concepts
- **Bias**: systematic decision distortion.
- **Journal**: the anti-bias instrument.

## Key Takeaways
1. Recognize emotion as chemistry, not information.
2. Write rules pre-trade; review them when you want to deviate.
3. Journal every trade with the "why" — bias shows up in patterns.
