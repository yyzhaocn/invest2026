# Chapter 1: The Trader's Edge

## Core Idea
An edge is positive expectancy that exceeds the "vig" (costs); most trades will lose — the edge is in asymmetric winners, structure understanding, and disciplined execution, not prediction.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Edge & Expectancy
- **Expectancy** = (win rate × avg win) − (loss rate × avg loss) − costs.
- A positive-expectancy system can still lose money to the **vig** (spread/commission/slippage) — edge must clear costs.
- Edge sources: market structure (trend/range), price action, pattern probabilities — implemented with discipline.

### Being Wrong Is Normal
- Most trades lose; survival requires sizing so a losing streak doesn't hurt.
- The edge is the *asymmetry*: losers are small, winners are large.

## Key Concepts
- **Edge**: repeatable statistical advantage.
- **Expectancy**: expected value per trade after costs.
- **The vig**: unavoidable transaction costs.

## Key Takeaways
1. Measure edge in expectancy after the vig, not win rate.
2. Accept that most trades lose — asymmetry is what pays.
3. Edge is earned through structure + discipline, not prediction skill.
