# Chapter 23: Trend from the Open and Small Pullback Trends

## Core Idea
Trend-from-the-open days form one extreme in the first few bars and trend all day, often closing at the opposite extreme. The small pullback trend is the strongest variant — pullbacks of only 10–30% of the average daily range, relentless all day.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Recognition**: Low of a bull trend day (high of a bear day) forms within the first few bars. Opening range < 25% of the average daily range → possible double bottom (bull) / double top (bear); ~50% → more likely a trending trading range day. Large gaps often lead to trend-from-the-open days (gap up → bull ~60% of the time; larger gap = more likely trend in the gap direction).
- **First Pullback Entry**: If the initial spike lasts 3+ bars, entering on the first pullback is usually good for at least a scalp. Strong moves have at least two legs — entering the first pullback capitalizes on the propensity to test the extreme. Use: this is THE entry for missed trend-from-the-open moves.
- **Small Pullback Trend Logic**: Pullbacks of 10–30% of average daily range; swing setups are 70%+ likely to work even though they never look it. Signal bars look bad (as in all strong trends). Many countertrend trend bars keep beginners looking for reversals. Use: buy every weak-looking bull flag; trail stops below swing lows, not to breakeven (breakeven stops get trapped out).
- **Midday Climax Tell**: ~2/3 of small pullback days have a larger pullback after 11:00 PST, often ~2× the biggest pullback so far, usually heralded by 1–2 relatively large trend bars (exhaustive climax). Use: exit longs / consider shorting the close of that second big bar, expecting a 3–5 point pullback, then re-enter with trend.
- **Four-Bar Rule**: If the market trends 4+ bars without a pullback (or two large trend bars), it's a strong spike → likely a spike-and-channel day; the spike is followed by: exhaustive climax (reversal risk), a tight range (trend resumption day), or most commonly a channel (spike and channel day).

## Key Concepts
- **20 gap bar / moving average gap bar**: late-trend setups on these days.
- **Opening range breakout**: first bar high/low breaks → day is "trend from the open" at least momentarily; most days fail the breakout and evolve elsewhere.
- **Report bars**: 7:00 PST reports can create reversal/breakout/outside bars — but computers have the edge on reports; wait for the always-in read a bar or two later.

## Mental Models
- Think of trend-from-the-open as "the strongest trends have the smallest pullbacks."
- Think of the midday big bars as the climax tell — strength at the end of a long trend is often exhaustion.
- Think of weak-looking flags as "the market's way of keeping you out."

## Anti-patterns
- **Chasing after missing the first pullback**: use the first-pullback entry logic instead (Ch 11 late-entry equivalence).
- **Moving stops to breakeven too eagerly**: pullbacks hit breakeven stops and trap you out; trail below swing lows.
- **Trading at the exact moment of a report**: computers have the edge; wait for the always-in read.
- **Buying above the first bar expecting a trend from the open**: trends-from-the-open develop in only ~20% of days; reversals are more common in the first hour.

## Worked Example
A gap down opens, then the market reverses up strongly from the first bars — a bull trend from the open day. Pullbacks all day are 2–3 points (avg daily range 12). Traders who missed the original entry buy the first pullback (high 1/2) with a stop below the pullback low; they trail below swing lows. Between 11:00 and noon, two relatively large consecutive bull trend bars break to a new high — the experienced read: exhaustive buy climax, not a new leg. Longs exit; aggressive traders short the close of the second bull bar expecting a 3–5 point pullback lasting ~10 bars, then re-buy the trend into the close.

## Key Takeaways
1. Trend-from-the-open: extreme in the first few bars; small opening range raises the odds.
2. Missed the move? Buy/sell the first pullback after a 3+ bar spike.
3. Small pullback trends: buy weak-looking flags, trail below swing lows (not breakeven).
4. Midday big bars after a long small-pullback trend = climax; expect a larger pullback.
