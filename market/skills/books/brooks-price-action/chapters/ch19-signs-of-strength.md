# Chapter 19: Signs of Strength in a Trend

## Core Idea
Strong trends announce themselves early through a long checklist of characteristics. The more signs present, the more you should focus exclusively on with-trend entries — and in the strongest trends you don't need a setup at all.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Strength Checklist** (the more present, the stronger the trend):
  - Big gap opening on the day.
  - Trending highs and lows (swings).
  - Most bars are trend bars in the trend's direction.
  - Little overlap of consecutive bar bodies (lows at or one tick below prior closes in bull spikes).
  - Bars with no/small tails = urgency.
  - Gaps between bodies; breakout gaps (strong trend bar at start); measuring gaps (pullback doesn't overlap breakout point); micro measuring gaps (strong bar with gap between prior and next bar).
  - No big climaxes; few large bars — the largest trend bars are often countertrend (trapping traders into the wrong direction).
  - No significant trend channel line overshoots; sideways corrections after trend line breaks.
  - Failed wedges and other failed reversals.
  - Sequence of 20 moving-average gap bars.
  - Few if any profitable countertrend trades; small, infrequent, mostly sideways pullbacks.
  - Weak signal bars (the trend keeps traders out, forcing chasing).
  - Repeated two-legged pullbacks with trend entries.
  - No two consecutive trend bar closes on the wrong side of the MA.
  - The trend breaks several resistance levels by many ticks.
  - Reversal spikes against the trend fail and become flags.
  Use: count the signs; ≥ threshold → only with-trend entries; start looking at countertrend setups only as with-trend entry locations (enter on stops where countertrend traders will be forced out).
- **No Setup Needed Rule**: Once you realize the market is in a strong trend, you can enter at the market anytime with a relatively small stop. "The only purpose of a setup is to minimize the risk." Use: in strong trends, don't wait for setups; they rarely come.

## Key Concepts
- **Trending anything**: closes, highs, lows, or bodies trending.
- **20 gap bars**: 20+ consecutive bars not touching the moving average.
- **Micro gap**: low of the bar after a strong trend bar at/above the high of the bar before it — a breakout test and sign of strength.

## Mental Models
- Think of strong trends as *trying to keep you out*: weak signals and countertrend-looking bars are features, not bugs.
- Think of countertrend setups in strong trends as "the best-looking traps" — the biggest trend bars are often countertrend.
- Think of the checklist as a filter: how many signs do you see right now?

## Anti-patterns
- **Looking for countertrend trades in a strong trend**: "The countertrend setups almost always look better than the with-trend setups" — that's the trap.
- **Waiting for strong signal bars in strong trends**: they mostly look bad; buy the pullback anyway or enter at market.
- **Doubting a trend because reversal attempts look scary**: 80% of reversal attempts fail and become flags.

## Worked Example
A strong bull trend day: big gap up, trending highs/lows, little body overlap, several micro gaps, pullbacks of only 3–4 points (avg range 12), the best-looking bar of the day is a large bear reversal bar that traps beginners into shorts, and the pullback signal bars are weak bull bars that most traders skip. Experienced traders buy the close of every weak signal bar and on limit orders below prior lows; they swing most of their position because the checklist says with-trend only. The trapped shorts fuel the continuation.

## Key Takeaways
1. Run the strength checklist before choosing your trading mode.
2. In strong trends, enter at market with a small stop — setups only minimize risk.
3. Weak signal bars and countertrend-looking bars are normal in strong trends.
4. Never let the best-looking (countertrend) setup talk you out of the trend.
