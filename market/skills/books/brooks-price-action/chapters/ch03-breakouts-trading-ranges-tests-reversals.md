# Chapter 3: Breakouts, Trading Ranges, Tests, and Reversals

## Core Idea
Breakouts are transitions from trading ranges into trends; tests are returns to key areas; most breakout attempts fail. The skill is distinguishing a successful breakout from a failed one (a reversal), and understanding that channels, tests, and reversals are all the same underlying machinery at different scales.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Spike-and-Channel Day Structure**: A breakout spike is followed by a pullback, then the trend resumes as a channel (more overlap, tails, countertrend bars). The start of the channel = start of an incipient trading range; the bottom of the channel is usually tested within a day or two. Use: after a spike, expect a channel, then a test of the channel's start.
- **Channel-as-Flag**: Treat every bull channel as a bear flag and every bear channel as a bull flag — channels usually get retraced. Use: don't chase channel breakouts; look for the eventual downside/upside breakout and fade it (or enter on the breakout pullback).
- **Test Framework**: A test returns to support/resistance (trend line, channel line, measured move, swing high/low, entry bar extreme, yesterday's levels). Behavior at the test decides the trade: strong breakout → buy one tick above the old high or on the pullback; weak test + reversal bar → short below the reversal bar. Use: always identify what price is testing; the test is where risk/reward sets up.
- **Reversal Framework**: Most "reversals" (trend→opposite trend) actually fail and become a trend→trading-range transition. But the move is usually big enough for a swing trade. Use: take partial profits at first reasonable targets — the opposite trend may never come.

## Key Concepts
- **Test**: return to an area of support or resistance with the intent to gauge commitment.
- **Breakout**: transition from two-sided (range) to one-sided (trend) behavior.
- **Failed breakout (reversal)**: breakout that fails to follow through and reverses — often trapping breakout traders.
- **Breakout strength factors**: large body, small tails, volume 10–20× average, spike 5–10 bars, breaks several levels by many ticks, first pullback doesn't reach the breakout point.

## Mental Models
- Think of every sharp move and every strong reversal as a *breakout of something*.
- Think "spike → pullback → channel → test of channel start" as the default anatomy of every trend.
- Think of ranges as magnets that pull breakouts back — most attempts fail.

## Anti-patterns
- **Buying the breakout of a channel**: the with-trend channel breakout usually fails within ~5 bars; wait for the pullback (or fade it).
- **Ignoring the risk and probability of a countertrend breakout trade**: beginners see only reward; the equation includes risk and probability — most breakouts fail.
- **Scaling into shorts in a channel late in the day**: you may run out of time to exit.

## Worked Example
(Figure 3.1, 5-min Emini) Market tests yesterday's low — the breakout down fails, forming a lower low, then reverses sharply up in a spike to bar 4 (both bulls and bears agree price is too low). Bar 5 tests the bar 2 signal bar high, forming a higher low. A channel up forms bars 5–6 (overlapping bars = hesitation, profit-taking). The market retraces to ~the channel start at bar 7 (higher-low test of bar 5's swing low; double bottom). Bar 8 makes a lower-high test of bar 6 and yesterday's close — a double top, not a breakout. The reversal down to bar 9 tests the moving average, misses the long's breakeven stop by a tick (bulls strong → new high follows), and the day breaks to a new high. Every swing was a test of something.

## Key Takeaways
1. Identify what price is testing at all times; the test outcome sets up the trade.
2. After a spike, expect a channel, then a test back to the channel's start.
3. Treat channels as flags — most channel breakouts fail within ~5 bars.
4. Most reversals only become trading ranges — take partial profits early.
