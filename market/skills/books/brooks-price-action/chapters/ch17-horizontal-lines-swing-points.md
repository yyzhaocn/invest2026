# Chapter 17: Horizontal Lines — Swing Points and Other Key Price Levels

## Core Idea
On most (non-trend) days, horizontal lines across swing highs and lows act as barriers: expect failed breakouts and reversals at them. On trend days, use horizontal lines only to enter on pullbacks.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Swing Line Failure Framework**: Expect swing high breakouts to fail and form higher-high reversal setups; swing low breakouts to fail and form lower-low reversals. Sometimes the failure fails → breakout pullback → a second, more extreme high/low. The fade of a *second* higher-high/lower-low is even more reliable (second signals are best on range days). Use: on range days, watch all prior swing highs/lows for failed breakouts → reversal entries.
- **Trend Day Rule**: On strong trend days, use horizontal lines only for pullback entries (e.g. breakout pullback buy at the old breakout level). Fading swing highs/lows countertrend on trend days requires first a good trend line break AND a strong reversal bar — and even then it's only a scalp. Use: don't let countertrend fades distract from with-trend swing entries.
- **Middle-of-Day Magnet**: The middle of the day acts like a magnet on range days; a second higher high/lower low is a more extreme point, so it's more likely to yield a scalper's profit. Use: prefer second signals over firsts.

## Key Concepts
- **Swing high/low**: prior pivot points — the "true" support/resistance.
- **Breakout pullback**: failed breakout → opposite setup; the second attempt.
- **Double bottom/top**: often just a higher low / lower high test of the swing.

## Mental Models
- Think of swing levels as "magnetic barriers" that attract tests and produce failed breakouts.
- Think "second signal > first signal" on range days.
- Think of trend days as different worlds: swing fades there are scalps at best.

## Anti-patterns
- **Fading swing highs/lows in strong trends without a trend line break + strong reversal bar**: countertrend scalps that distract you from swing entries.
- **Taking first signals on range days**: second signals are the reliable ones.
- **Trailing stops below swing lows in trading-range conditions** (see Ch 22): they get hit.

## Worked Example
(Figure 17.1) A range day: bar 5 is a second higher high over bar 2 (and a third push up from the bar 3 low — three-push patterns behave like wedges). Bar 9 is a second attempt to reverse up after falling below the open's low, a double bottom with yesterday's low. Bar 13 is a large double bottom pullback (high 2 buy at the bottom of two complex legs down) and a failed breakout of the bar 9–11 bull channel (bear flag). Bar 15 is a double top bear flag that fails as a large bull breakout bar goes above its high. Bar 17: a second lower low below bar 14 — a wedge bull flag and breakout pullback to a higher low. Every swing was a test; second signals worked repeatedly.

## Key Takeaways
1. On range days, swing highs/lows produce failed breakouts → reversal entries; prefer second signals.
2. On trend days, horizontal lines are for pullback entries only.
3. A second higher-high/lower-low (middle-of-day magnet) is more reliable than a first.
4. Three-push patterns behave like wedges even without the shape.
