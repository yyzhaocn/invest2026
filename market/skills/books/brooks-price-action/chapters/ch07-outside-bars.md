# Chapter 7: Outside Bars

## Core Idea
An outside bar has its high above the prior bar's high AND its low below the prior bar's low — both bulls and bears controlled it at some point. It is by definition a one-bar trading range, which makes it tricky: it can act as a reversal bar, a trend bar, or meaningless noise depending on context.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Outside Bar Context Rules**:
  - In the middle of a trading range: meaningless — both sides balanced; don't generate trades from it.
  - As an entry bar after a good signal bar: reliable (e.g. buy stop left in after a bull reversal bar gets filled on a reversal back up, making an outside bar).
  - As a second entry in a strong reversal from a trend line break or channel overshoot: can be an excellent entry bar.
  - With-trend outside bar in the first leg of a trend reversal: functions like a strong trend bar, not a range bar.
  Use: judge the context first; "the single most important thing is that when uncertain, wait for more price action."
- **Outside Bar Trap Mechanics**: Aggressive bulls buy below the prior bar's low (not waiting for above its high) → the bar extends above the prior high → outside up bar → bears who shorted below the prior low are trapped → they cover → one-sided move up → two legs up from the *higher low* (not the actual low, because that's where agreement formed).
- **ioi Pattern**: outside bar followed by inside bar = inside-outside-inside. Breakout of the inside bar can be a setup, but only if the move can reach your target; avoid when the inside bar is large and near the outside bar's middle (barbwire).

## Key Concepts
- **Outside bar**: high > prior high AND low < prior low.
- **Trapped bears/bulls**: the fuel for the post-trap trend.
- **Breakout pullback**: a failed breakout that fails → the opposite setup.
- **Barbwire**: tight trading range — avoid ioi breakouts there.

## Mental Models
- Think of an outside bar as "a fight both sides lost and won" — the close tells you who ended up on top.
- Think of the trap: institutions *engineer* the bear-flag short trigger so longs get trapped out and new shorts get trapped in, then buy aggressively.
- Think "outside bar = one-bar trading range; don't buy the top or sell the bottom of a range."

## Anti-patterns
- **Entering on the breakout of a large 5-min outside bar**: the distant stop is too risky; almost always unwise.
- **Using outside bars to generate trades in the middle of ranges**: noise.
- **Shorting below a bear reversal bar that's really a two-bar/trading-range pattern**: you'd be shorting at support (see overlap rule, Ch 5).

## Key Takeaways
1. Outside bars are one-bar ranges — context decides whether to trade them at all.
2. Best use: entry bar after a good signal bar, or second-entry reversal from trend line/channel overshoot.
3. Outside-bar traps (buying below prior lows) produce strong two-legged moves from the higher low.
4. When unsure what an outside bar means, wait for more price action.
