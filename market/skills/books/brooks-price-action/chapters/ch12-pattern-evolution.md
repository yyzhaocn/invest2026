# Chapter 12: Pattern Evolution

## Core Idea
Patterns morph into other patterns, fail, and evolve into larger ones — and that's normal. Reliable patterns fail about 40% of the time. Read what's in front of you now; don't trade the pattern that completed a couple of bars ago.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Failure as the Core Mechanism**: A pattern that fails to yield a scalper's profit reverses into a signal in the other direction. Trapped traders exiting with losses fuel the opposite move. Use: when a setup fails, expect the reversal to be tradable — the trapped traders are the fuel.
- **Evolution Paths**:
  - Expanding triangle → 7 legs instead of 5.
  - Micro trend line breakout fails → breakout pullback.
  - Final bear flag fails → breakout pullback sell → often enlarges into a wedge reversal or larger range (larger final flag).
  - Bull spike and channel → trading range → double bottom bull flag.
  - In the first hour: double top bear flags often evolve into double bottom bull flags (and vice versa).
  Use: anticipate the next morph; take both entries when a big move is likely (first or second pattern).
- **Breakout Mode in the First Hour**: It's common to see both a double top and a double bottom in the first hour — the market is in breakout mode. One extreme usually forms in the first hour; the market then moves away from it for the next couple of hours. Use: take both entries and swing part, because a big move commonly follows one of them.

## Key Concepts
- **Morphed pattern**: a pattern that changes into another (e.g. low 2 → failed low 2 → spike and channel top).
- **Breakout pullback**: a failed breakout's failure — the second attempt, often the real entry.
- **Trapped traders**: the fuel of the evolved pattern's move.

## Mental Models
- Think of every pattern as provisional — "names are never important."
- Think of a failure as a *transfer of fuel*: from trapped traders to the opposite move.
- Think of the first hour as decision time: patterns there often reverse into their mirror image.

## Anti-patterns
- **Trading the pattern that already completed**: read the current bar and context.
- **Insisting on perfect labels**: a wedge without a perfect wedge shape still behaves like a wedge (three-push patterns behave like wedges).
- **Missing the second entry after a first failure**: the evolved pattern is often the better trade.

## Worked Example
(Figure 12.2) First hour, GS: a double top bear flag forms at bar 4 at the falling moving average (low 2 short). The market then reverses up at bar 5 into a double bottom bull flag. Both entries were takable: short below bar 4, then long above bar 5 — and swing part, because a big move commonly follows either. GS ran $3 up to the bar 6 high of the day. The bull spikes up from bars 1 and 5 were stronger than the bear spikes down from bars 2 and 4, so the odds favored a bull channel.

## Key Takeaways
1. Patterns fail ~40% of the time — treat failure as information, not surprise.
2. When a pattern fails, look for the opposite signal; trapped traders fuel it.
3. First-hour double tops/bottoms often morph into each other — take both, swing part.
4. Names don't matter; read the current price action.
