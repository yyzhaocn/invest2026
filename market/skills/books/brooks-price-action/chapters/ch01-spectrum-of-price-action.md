# Chapter 1: The Spectrum of Price Action

## Core Idea
The market exists on a spectrum from extreme trend (almost every tick higher/lower) to extreme trading range (every up-tick followed by a down-tick), and it oscillates between them. Almost all price action — and every chart you will ever look at — is some mixture of these two states.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Trend vs. Trading Range Spectrum**: Every segment of a chart is either trending (one side dominant) or two-sided (bulls/bears alternate control). All trends contain smaller trading ranges; all trading ranges contain smaller trends. Most trends are parts of trading ranges on higher time frames (HTF) and vice versa. Use: classify the current regime before deciding anything else.
- **Market Inertia**: The market tends to continue doing what it has just been doing. In a trend, most reversal attempts fail. In a trading range, most breakout attempts fail. Use: default to betting on continuation of the current regime; require strong evidence to bet on change.
- **Two-Legged Moves**: Moves tend to subdivide into two legs. Traditional names (ABC, AB=CD, Elliott waves) are inconsistent — Brooks prefers counting legs (High 1, High 2, Low 1, Low 2) because it handles 3rd and 4th legs cleanly.

## Key Concepts
- **Trend bar / trading range bar**: At the bar level, a bar is either trending (body away from open) or a doji (equilibrium).
- **High 1 / High 2 / Low 1 / Low 2**: In a pullback, the first bar whose high exceeds the prior bar's high ends the first down-leg (High 1); second occurrence is High 2. Mirror for Low 1/2 in rallies.
- **ABC move**: A two-legged pullback in a trend; the breakout from the C leg is the entry.
- **Always-in**: (intro) the position you'd hold if forced to be in the market all day; the core trend read.

## Mental Models
- Think of a chart as "trending, ranging, or transitioning" — never just "up or down."
- Think of every pullback as a *leg count*: the count tells you when the pullback is likely exhausted.
- Think of the market as having memory (inertia): change is expensive and usually fails the first time.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating "trend" and "range" as opposites**: they are alternating states that morph into each other.
- **Assuming a 5th day up is more likely to reverse**: markets with inertia trend longer than intuition expects.
- **Using AB=CD / Elliott terminology**: inconsistent and useless for counting 3rd/4th legs.

## Worked Example
A day that opens with a strong bear trend down to bar 1, then an unusually tight trading range, breaks out up one tick at bar 2 (a failed lower-low test), then reverses into a strong downside breakout trending to bar 3. The two legs down = leg 1 (to bar 1) and leg 2 (to bar 3) separated by the failed breakout at bar 2. The breakout attempt up failed — inertia kept the market bearish. A trader counting legs would have entered short at the failed upside breakout (the second push), not at the original bottom.

## Key Takeaways
1. Classify the regime first: trending, trading range, or transition.
2. Bet on inertia: trends resist reversal; ranges resist breakout.
3. Count pullback legs (High/Low 1, 2, 3) to time with-trend entries.
4. Every swing is a test of *something* — usually a prior swing point, trend line, or HTF level.
