# Chapter 26: Stairs — Broad Channel Trend

## Core Idea
A stairs day is a variant of a trending trading range day with at least three trading ranges: broad swings with trending highs and lows, where each breakout is followed by a pullback (breakout test) that overlaps the breakout point.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Recognition**: 3+ trending swings resembling a mildly sloping range/channel. Each pullback retraces beyond its breakout point (overlap between breakout spike and following pullback). Two-way trading is present → entries in both directions, but swing part/all of with-trend trades.
- **Shrinking Stairs = Waning Momentum**: If each breakout is smaller than the prior, it's a shrinking stairs pattern — a sign of waning momentum that often leads to a two-legged reversal and trend line break. Use: when you see shrinking stairs, prepare for the reversal (or at least stop adding with-trend).
- **Breakout Size as a Template**: Traders measure how many ticks breakouts run past the most recent swing point, then use that number to fade subsequent breakouts expecting a breakout test. E.g. if the last swing low fell 14 ticks below the prior one, scale into longs from ~10 ticks below the most recent swing low (near the trend channel line); if pullbacks were ~15 ticks, take profits ~10–15 ticks up (near the trend line). Use: the measured ticks become your entry/profit template — experienced only.
- **Acceleration Variant**: One stair can accelerate and break the trend channel with-trend. If it reverses, expect at least a two-legged move; if not, expect ~2 more legs or a measured move equal to ~the channel height.

## Key Concepts
- **Breakout test**: every breakout is followed by a pullback beyond the breakout point.
- **Shrinking stairs**: smaller and smaller breakouts — momentum decay.
- **Stairs as flags**: stairs are often pullbacks/flags in higher time frame trends — a broad bull channel today may be a large bear flag that breaks out tomorrow.

## Mental Models
- Think of stairs as "a range that keeps trying to trend" — buy the lows, sell the highs, but respect the slope.
- Think of the tick template as the institution's yardstick: they size orders and targets from prior breakout distances.
- Think of shrinking stairs as a warning light, not a reversal signal — wait for the actual trend line break + two-legged reversal.

## Anti-patterns
- **Beginners using limit-order scalps in stairs**: "this style of trading is only for experienced traders. Beginners should restrict themselves to stop entries."
- **Assuming three pushes down = reversal**: "Three pushes down does not guarantee a trend reversal" — without strong climactic action, the pattern just becomes a wedge bear flag and the trend resumes.
- **Adding with-trend into shrinking stairs**: you're buying the momentum decay.

## Worked Example
(Figure 26.1) A bear stairs day: each breakout to a new low is followed by a rally back above the breakout point but below the prior swing high. The low of bar 5 was ~4 points below the low of bar 3; aggressive bulls placed limit orders 3–4 points below bar 5's low — unfilled on the sell-off to bar 7, filled at bar 9 (4 points below bar 7's low). Since prior rallies were ~4 points, they took profits ~3 points above entries, repeating at bars 11 and 16. Bears did the opposite: rallies were 4–6 points, so they scaled shorts 3–5 points above the most recent swing low near the bear trend line. Bar 7 was a third push down and a shrinking stair (less extension than bar 5) — but the move down had little buying pressure and no strong climactic reversals, so it became a wedge bear flag and the bear trend resumed.

## Key Takeaways
1. Stairs: 3+ ranges with trending swings; trade both directions, swing the with-trend side.
2. Shrinking stairs = waning momentum; watch for a two-legged reversal.
3. Use the prior breakout distances (ticks) as your entry/profit template — experienced only.
4. Three pushes down ≠ reversal without strong price action; often just a wedge flag.
