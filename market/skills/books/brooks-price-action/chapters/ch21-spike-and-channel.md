# Chapter 21: Spike and Channel Trend

## Core Idea
The spike and channel is the most common trend type — present in some form almost every day and in every trend. Every trend has a spike phase (urgent, one-sided) and a channel phase (two-sided, "wall of worry"), and the market is always in one of the two modes.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Spike Phase**: One or more trend bars, a breakaway gap into a clear always-in situation. Sense of urgency; traders add as they press. Usually forms in the first hour, often in the first few bars. The spike ends at the first pause/pullback. Use: trade the spike aggressively — buy closes of trend bars in the spike, enter at market; you must have at least a small position.
- **Channel Phase**: After the pullback, the trend resumes with less strength: more overlap, flatter slope, countertrend bars, pullbacks. The channel is a *wall of worry* — traders quick to take profits but keep reentering. Use: trade the channel like a trending range — buy below the low of the prior bar, hold part for swing; shorts are scalps only.
- **Channel Endings**: The channel ends at a measured move target and often on a third push. The with-trend channel breakout usually fails within ~5 bars and reverses — don't enter on that breakout; wait for the pullback (e.g. short the lower high after a bull channel breaks down). The market usually corrects to the start of the channel (a test of the spike/gap), then reverses at least 25% in the trend direction.
- **Strong Breakout → Measured Move**: The stronger the breakout, the more likely a channel and the further it goes; the spike's height often anchors a measured move target for taking partial/complete profits.

## Key Concepts
- **Spike = gap**: spikes, climaxes, and breakouts are the same thing; the pullback after the spike is a gap test.
- **Second spike**: rare; usually becomes a failed breakout followed by correction.
- **Weak variant**: a weak spike and channel is probably a trending trading range day (Ch 22).
- **Fractal nesting**: smaller spike-and-channels nest inside larger ones; a spike on one frame is a steep tight channel on a smaller frame.

## Mental Models
- Think of the spike as "everyone agrees" and the channel as "nobody is sure but it keeps going."
- Think of the channel breakout as the classic trap: it fails within ~5 bars.
- Think "spike = trade aggressively; channel = trade the range, expect the correction to the channel start."

## Anti-patterns
- **Chasing the channel breakout** (with-trend): usually fails within 5 bars; wait for the pullback.
- **Trading the channel as a strong trend**: it's two-sided — respect the range behavior.
- **Fading the spike too early**: urgency means the move can go much further; the spike is not overdone until the first pause bar.

## Worked Example
A day gaps up on news. Bar 1 is a huge bull trend bar (the spike), followed by two more strong bars — a clear always-in long. The first pullback comes after 5 bars (a gap test). The trend resumes as a channel: overlapping bars, small pullbacks, occasional bear bars. Traders buy below prior bar lows and hold part for a swing; the channel reaches a measured move target on a third push and a strong bear reversal bar forms at the trend channel line overshoot. The market corrects to the start of the channel, tests the gap, then bounces at least 25% back in the trend direction as it forms a trading range — often a double bottom bull flag with the channel bottom.

## Key Takeaways
1. Every trend = spike phase + channel phase; know which you're in at all times.
2. Trade the spike aggressively (closes/at market); trade the channel as a trending range.
3. The with-trend channel breakout usually fails within ~5 bars — don't chase it.
4. Expect a correction to the channel start (gap test), then ≥25% back in the trend direction.
