# Chapter 24: Reversal Day

## Core Idea
A reversal day trends one direction, then trends the opposite into the close. Most start as trending trading range days. The strongest new trends sometimes begin mid-day or later — a clear always-in flip you must enter quickly, even if it looks climactic.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Always-In Flip**: Some of the strongest trends begin mid-day/end-of-day as runaway trend mode: large trend bars, little overlap, small tails, a breakout and clear always-in flip. Use: enter quickly and swing most of your position — "even if the new trend looks climactic and overdone (and it is, but it will likely continue to get much more so!)."
- **Countertrend Spike Tell**: A pullback that begins with a strong countertrend spike might be a reversal, not a flag: the pullback grows into a trend channel in the opposite direction. If a "two-legged rally to the moving average" in a bear day begins with a fairly strong bull trend bar, consider that it could be a bull spike followed by a relentless bull channel. Use: when you see a strong countertrend spike, restrict trading to the new direction — you'll lose money hoping the old trend resumes.
- **Trending Trading Range Day Origin**: Most reversal days begin as trending trading range days — whenever you recognize one, be prepared for a possible late reversal swing that could cover the whole day's range.
- **Profit-Taking Reality**: Pullbacks happen because bulls take profits even in strong trends — no trend is immune to a deep pullback. Use: take partial profits; don't let a big profit become a loss waiting for perfection.

## Key Concepts
- **Reversal day**: trends one way, then the other into the close.
- **Breakout spike**: the always-in flip requires a strong breakout/spike.
- **Lower highs/lows tell**: between the old trend's end and the reversal, watch for lower highs and lows (bearish) or higher lows/highs (bullish).

## Mental Models
- Think of the reversal day as "the trend flipped when you weren't looking — believe the new direction."
- Think of the countertrend spike as a fork: flag or reversal? The spike's strength decides.
- Think "losing on every trade = the market is trending the wrong way and you don't believe it."

## Anti-patterns
- **Holding shorts hoping the morning bear trend resumes** after a strong countertrend spike: the reversal is eating your account.
- **Scaling/scalping the old direction after the flip**: "if you continued to take long scalps, you would eventually realize you were losing on every trade."
- **Waiting for a pullback to enter the new trend**: when the always-in flips with a runaway spike, you must enter quickly, even at market.

## Worked Example
(Figure 24.1) A sharp two-legged move up in the first hour, then a tight channel pullback to the moving average, then sideways for over two hours — the last profitable long entry was 8:50 a.m. at bar 5. The market breaks down at bar 9 into a lower range: a strong bear spike (breakaway gap), then a perfect measured move into the close. Traders who kept taking long scalps lost on every trade — the sign the market had flipped. Bar 9's spike down was followed by a channel; the correct trade was short the breakout pullback and swing into the close, trailing above prior swing highs.

## Key Takeaways
1. When the always-in flips mid-day, enter quickly and swing most of your position.
2. A strong countertrend spike in a pullback may be the start of a reversal channel — stop trading the old direction.
3. Most reversal days begin as trending trading range days — watch for the late flip.
4. Losing on every trade = you're on the wrong side; believe the chart, not your hope.
