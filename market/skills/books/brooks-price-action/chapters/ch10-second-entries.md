# Chapter 10: Second Entries

## Core Idea
A second entry is almost always more likely to profit than a first entry. In strong moves, never trade the first reversal attempt — wait for the market's second attempt to reverse.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Second Entry > First Entry**: Bottoms and tops need a second reversal off the low/high to convince enough traders. Use: prefer the second entry in any reversal or breakout context.
- **Good Fill = Bad Trade**: Most good second entries come at the same price or *worse* than the first — a second-entry trader is entering late and pays a little more for the added information. If the market lets you in *better*, you're probably missing something. Use: be suspicious of bargain fills; "most of the time, a good fill equals a bad trade (and a bad fill equals a good trade!)."
- **Wait for Second Reversals in Strong Moves**: Fading a move after 4+ consecutive bull trend bars (or 2–3 large ones) is entering against too much momentum. Wait for the first reversal attempt to fail, the trend to resume a bar or two, then enter on the market's second attempt. Use: countertrend only on second signals with evidence the bulls moved more than a couple of ticks above the prior bar.
- **Micro Double Tops/Bottoms**: Most tops come from micro double tops; most bottoms from micro double bottoms — the second push is the test of the reversal.

## Key Concepts
- **Second entry**: the market's second attempt at a reversal or breakout.
- **Trapped momentum**: the first reversal's traders, whose exits fuel the second move.
- **Breakout test**: when the second attempt tests the prior extreme.

## Mental Models
- Think of the first attempt as reconnaissance: it tells you where the trapped traders are.
- Think of price as information: a bargain fill means the market is telling you something you don't see.
- Think "second signal" = the pattern that separates winners from beginners.

## Anti-patterns
- **Shorting the first reversal after a strong bull spike**: too much momentum; the first attempt almost always fails.
- **Celebrating a better fill on a second entry**: it's usually a trap ("good fill, bad trade").
- **Taking countertrend trades without prior evidence of countertrend strength in the preceding bars.

## Key Takeaways
1. Prefer second entries over first entries, in everything.
2. Expect second entries at the same or worse price; distrust better fills.
3. In strong moves, wait for the second reversal attempt before going countertrend.
4. Most tops/bottoms are micro double tops/bottoms — the second push tests the extreme.
