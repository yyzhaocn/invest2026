# Chapter 20: Two Legs

## Core Idea
The market regularly tries to do something twice. All moves — trends, pullbacks, reversals — subdivide into two legs. If the market fails in two attempts, it usually tries the opposite; if it succeeds, it often extends the trend.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Two-Leg Principle**: Any new trend, capitulation, or reversal will usually have at least a two-legged move — both sides need a second test before conviction. Use: after a trend line break or capitulation, expect leg 1, a pullback, then leg 2 to test the extreme; enter with the trend on leg 2's breakout or fade a failing second attempt.
- **Two-Legged Pullback (ABC)**: A countertrend move (A), a small with-trend move (B), then a second countertrend move deeper than the first (C). Use: the C leg is where with-trend entries set up (High 2 / Low 2).
- **Two-Legged Trend Structure (vs. Elliott)**: Look at the first strong with-trend leg as the start of momentum; it subdivides into two smaller legs; after a pullback, two more pushes test the extreme. Brooks: this two-legged view offers sound logic and abundant trades, unlike Elliott Wave theory which is "essentially useless to the vast majority of traders."
- **Two-Push vs. Three-Push Overlap**: Sometimes both legs exceed the prior extreme (a clear three-push pattern); other times only the second leg exceeds (a two-legged higher high at a top). Some "two-legged" moves have one leg made of two smaller legs — the composite leg compares in strength/duration to the single-leg one. Use: don't demand perfect patterns — "you are always in a gray fog."

## Key Concepts
- **Leg**: a change in direction of movement; every larger leg is made of smaller legs.
- **Breakout test**: the second leg's test of the trend extreme.
- **Double top/bottom**: second push testing a prior reversal point — if it fails there again, reversal/pullback likely.

## Mental Models
- Think "twice, then opposite or extension": the two-attempt logic is the backbone of double tops/bottoms and ABC corrections.
- Think of Elliott Wave as decorative; the two-legged count is operational.
- Think of unclear setups as skippable: "if you are not confident about your read, do not take the trade — there will always be another trade."

## Anti-patterns
- **Trading perfect-pattern hunts**: perfect patterns don't exist in a gray fog; unclear setups lose accounts.
- **Using Elliott Wave for entries**: useless for most traders trying to make money.
- **Checking higher time frame charts for confirmation**: diverting attention increases missed trades; one chart is enough.

## Worked Example
In a two-legged rally in a bear trend: bulls take profits above the first leg; new bears short; bears who shorted the first up-leg add to shorts when the second leg exceeds the first leg's high. If the shorts overwhelm the breakout buyers, the market goes down into a range or a new bear phase. The second leg is where the trade decision is made: fade it (bearish continuation) or respect the failed breakout (bullish reversal) — the context of the trend decides.

## Key Takeaways
1. Expect two legs in every move: trend, pullback, reversal, breakout.
2. ABC pullbacks set up with-trend entries at the C leg (High 2 / Low 2).
3. Prefer the two-legged count over Elliott Wave for trading decisions.
4. Skip unclear setups — the gray fog is normal; patience beats forcing.
