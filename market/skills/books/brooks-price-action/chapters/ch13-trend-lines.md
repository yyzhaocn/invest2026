# Chapter 13: Trend Lines

## Core Idea
A bull trend line is drawn across the lows of a bull trend; a bear trend line across the highs of a bear trend. Trend lines are for finding with-trend entries on pullbacks — and for identifying when control changes after a break.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Trend Line Purpose**: Most profitable trades are in the direction of the trend line until it breaks. Every time price pulls back to the line (even undershooting/overshooting), look for a reversal off the line and enter with the trend. Use: buy at/just below the bull trend line on sharp sell-offs with limit orders — the market must overshoot slightly to "find information" (test whether more buyers or sellers exist there).
- **Trend Line Break = First Sign of Two-Sided Control**: The single most important fact about a break: the market is no longer controlled by one side. After a break, expect a test of the trend's extreme (higher high or lower high), then trend continuation, reversal, or range. Use: treat a break as a warning, not a reversal signal.
- **Flattening Slope Progression**: Each successive trend line is flatter — the trend is losing momentum. Eventually trend lines in the opposite direction become more important; that's when control has switched. Use: draw each new line off the new swing point; watch the slope decay.
- **Repeated Tests**: If price tests a line many times in few bars, it will usually break through and attempt reversal — but sometimes it accelerates away instead. Use: don't assume the break direction; watch the breakout.

## Key Concepts
- **Failed breakout (the recurring theme)**: most breakouts fail — the market races at the line with strong momentum, then reverses. Beginners chase; experienced traders fade or enter on the breakout pullback.
- **One-bar trend**: a gap opening or large trend bar is effectively a one-bar trend/breakout — expect it to fail often; be ready to fade with a setup.
- **Trend line as distraction**: don't keep lines on the chart — draw, verify the test, erase. "You don't want lines on your chart for more than a few seconds."

## Mental Models
- Think of a trend line as "the market's memory of where buyers/sellers re-enter."
- Think of each break as a relay: control passes from one side to the other over several breaks, not one.
- Think of the countertrend trade as a three-part equation: reward, risk, probability — beginners price only the reward.

## Anti-patterns
- **Chasing strong momentum into the trend line** (selling strong sell-offs near the bull line): beginners short there; experienced traders buy at/just below.
- **Keeping drawn lines on the chart**: distraction; draw, test, erase.
- **Assuming a trend line break = reversal**: it's the first sign of two-sided trading; usually an extreme test follows first.

## Worked Example
(Figure 13.2) The market trends up with strong sell-offs that quickly drop to the bull trend line. Beginners short above/at/below the line believing a new bear trend is starting — they see only the reward. Experienced traders have limit orders to buy at and just below the line; the market usually goes a little below to find information, then resumes the bull trend — often after a big break below the line and a rally that tests the old bull high with a higher or lower high.

## Key Takeaways
1. Buy pullbacks to the bull trend line; sell rallies to the bear trend line.
2. A trend line break means two-sided trading is beginning — expect a test of the extreme.
3. Successive lines flatten as momentum decays; opposite lines take over when control flips.
4. Most breakouts fail — evaluate reward, risk, AND probability.
