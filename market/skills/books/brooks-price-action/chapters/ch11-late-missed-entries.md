# Chapter 11: Late and Missed Entries

## Core Idea
If you look at a chart and know you'd still be holding the swing portion of a trade had you entered at the original signal, enter at the market *now* with exactly the swing-size position you would still be holding — same stop, same logic.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Late Entry Equivalence**: Entering late at the market with the swing-size position and original stop is *logically identical* to having held the swing from the original entry. The open profit illusion ("someone else's money") is false — it's your money and the risk is identical. Use: enter without hesitation when the always-in position is clear and you'd still be in the trade.
- **Sizing Rule for Late Entries**: Enter only with the number of shares/contracts you would still be holding after the original entry (e.g. 100 of 300 shares), with the same stop you'd have had (e.g. breakeven or ~10 cents below the signal bar high). Your stop is usually larger than a scalp's, so trade smaller to keep dollar risk constant.
- **Consecutive Trend Bar Rule**: Once the market forms 4+ consecutive bull trend bars (not too large, so not climactic), buy at least a small position at the market instead of waiting for a pullback. Use: don't let a missed entry turn into a missed trend.

## Key Concepts
- **Swing portion**: the part of the position you'd hold for the bigger move.
- **Trailing stop from the original entry**: apply the stop you'd have used, then trail.
- **Adding on pauses/pullbacks**: after the late entry, add on pauses and move the whole position's stop up to the last signal bar.

## Mental Models
- Think of "missing the entry" as a non-event: the trade still exists at the market.
- Think of the always-in read as the only thing that matters for a late entry decision.
- Think of emotion ("I should have entered earlier") as the enemy — the math says enter now.

## Anti-patterns
- **Refusing to enter late because of the missed entry**: you lose the trade twice (missed entry + missed trend).
- **Entering late with the full original size**: your stop is bigger; keep dollar risk constant by scaling down.
- **Chasing with a scalp mindset**: you'll be stopped by normal pullbacks; use the swing stop.

## Worked Example
(Figure 11.1) GS: strong two-legged sell-off into the close yesterday, strong bull reversal bar at bar 2 setting up a higher-low test and bull trend day. A trader starting to watch around bar 4 sees a series of bull trend bars and a strong trend. If they normally trade 300 shares and would have 100 left from the original entry above bar 3, they buy 100 at the market with the same stop (breakeven or ~10 cents below the bar 3 high). They add on pauses (e.g. above bar 6), moving the whole position's stop to one tick below the bar 6 signal bar, trailing up.

## Key Takeaways
1. Late entry with the swing-size portion + original stop = identical to having held it.
2. After 4+ consecutive (non-climactic) trend bars, buy a small position at market.
3. Scale down size as your stop scales up; keep dollar risk constant.
4. Add on pauses/pullbacks and trail the entire position's stop.
