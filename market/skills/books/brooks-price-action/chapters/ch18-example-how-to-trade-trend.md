# Chapter 18: Example of How to Trade a Trend

## Core Idea
In a trend, the simple existence of the trend is reason enough to enter at least a small position at the market. Beyond that, there is a structured menu of stop-based and limit-based entries — the chapter is the practical playbook for trend trading.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Stop-Entry Menu (with trend)**:
  - Buy high 2 pullback to the moving average in a bull trend / sell low 2 in a bear trend.
  - Buy wedge bull flag pullback / sell wedge bear flag pullback.
  - Buy breakout pullback after a breakout from a bull flag / sell after a bear flag.
  - Buy high 1 pullback in a strong bull spike (but NOT after a buy climax) / sell low 1 in a strong bear spike (not after a sell climax).
  - In a very strong trend: buy on a stop above a prior swing high / sell below a prior swing low.
  Use: this is the default with-trend entry ladder — work top-down by trend strength.
- **Limit/Market-Entry Menu (experienced)**:
  - Buy a bull spike at the market, at the close of every bull trend bar in the spike, or on a limit at/below the low of the prior bar (wide stop needed).
  - Buy the close of the first bear bar in a bull spike / sell the close of the first bull bar in a bear spike.
  - Buy at a bull trend line or prior swing low (potential double bottom bull flag).
  - Buy at/below a low 1 or low 2 weak signal bar on a limit in a possible new bull trend after a strong reversal up.
  - Buy at/below the prior bar on a limit in a quiet bull flag at the moving average.
  - Buy below a bull bar that breaks above a bull flag, anticipating a breakout pullback.
  Use: limit entries require more experience — you're entering against the immediate move; stops are safer for beginners.
- **Breakout Test Add**: When swinging in a bull trend, buy (or add) on a breakout test — an attempt to run breakeven stops from an earlier long entry. Use: the test of your entry area is an opportunity, not a threat.

## Key Concepts
- **With-trend setup ladder**: high 2 → wedge flag → breakout pullback → high 1 in spike → stop above swing high (as trend strengthens).
- **Spike entries**: at-market/limit entries in the spike; wide stops required.
- **Breakout test**: revisit of the entry area running breakeven stops.

## Mental Models
- Think of the menu as "choose the entry style by trend strength and your experience."
- Think "any reason to enter is enough in a trend — the setup only minimizes risk."
- Think of stops vs. limits as a risk tradeoff: stops confirm the move; limits get better price but require reading.

## Anti-patterns
- **Waiting for a perfect setup in a trend**: "the simple existence of a trend is reason enough to enter at least a small position at the market."
- **Buying a high 1 after a buy climax** (and low 1 after a sell climax) — the climax invalidates the simple pullback count.
- **Beginners using limit entries in spikes**: wide stops + speed = difficult; use stops.

## Worked Example
A bull trend day develops. Entries taken down the menu: (1) buy the high 2 pullback at the moving average with a bull signal bar; (2) add on the wedge bull flag pullback; (3) after the breakout from the flag, buy the breakout pullback; (4) when the trend spikes, buy the close of the first bear bar in the bull spike with a wider stop; (5) on the breakout test of the entry area, add for the swing, moving the whole position's stop under the last swing low.

## Key Takeaways
1. In a trend, enter a small position at market — the trend itself is the setup.
2. Use the stop-entry ladder: high 2 → wedge flag → breakout pullback → high 1 (no climax) → stop above swing high.
3. Limit entries (spikes, flags, trend lines) are for experienced traders; stops for beginners.
4. Add on the breakout test of your entry area when swinging.
