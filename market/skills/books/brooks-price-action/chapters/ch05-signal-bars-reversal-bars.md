# Chapter 5: Signal Bars — Reversal Bars

## Core Idea
A reversal bar reverses some aspect of the prior bar(s) and is one of the most reliable signal bars. On a smaller time frame it is always a two-bar reversal: a climax bar followed by a breakout bar in the opposite direction. But shape alone is not enough — context and overlap decide whether it's a signal or a trap.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Reversal Bar Minimums**:
  - Bull reversal bar: minimum = close above open OR close above midpoint.
  - Best bull bars: open near/below prior close + close above open and prior close; lower tail ~1/3–1/2 of bar height with small upper tail; little overlap with prior bars; strong entry bar follow-through; close reverses the closes/highs of more than one bar.
  - Bear mirror: close below open or below midpoint; upper tail 1/3–1/2; close below prior closes/lows.
  Use: grade reversal bars against these criteria before entering countertrend.
- **Overlap Rule**: If a reversal bar largely overlaps prior bars, or its tail extends only a tick or two beyond, it's part of a trading range — nothing to reverse. Overlap guideline: if the midpoint of a bull reversal bar is above the prior bar's low, overlap may be excessive. Use: reject reversal signals in overlap-heavy ranges; wait for a second signal.
- **Test-of-Extreme Rule**: Before a countertrend reversal trade, demand a trend line break AND a strong reversal bar on the test of the extreme. The test matters because the market tries twice: if sellers fail on the second attempt at the low, it likely rallies. Use: double tops/bottoms work because of this two-attempt logic.
- **Reversal Bar Failure Pattern**: In strong trends a good-looking reversal bar can fail in the final seconds before close (price collapses to the low). Use: watch the close — a collapsed close turns the signal into a trap that fuels the trend.

## Key Concepts
- **Bull reversal bar**: bar reversing down-move; lower tail = rejected selling.
- **Bear reversal bar**: bar reversing up-move; upper tail = rejected buying.
- **Large doji**: one-bar trading range — rarely a good reversal signal; wait for a second signal.
- **Trapped traders**: the fuel that drives the post-trap move; every reversal signal implies someone is trapped.

## Mental Models
- Think of a reversal bar as a *claim* the market must back up with follow-through.
- Think "reversal = climax + breakout" on some time frame, even if not visible on yours.
- Think of countertrend as low-probability: everything must look perfect, including the signal bar.

## Anti-patterns
- **Trading 1-minute reversal bars countertrend**: "you bleed to death from a thousand paper cuts" — most fail and become with-trend setups.
- **Buying at the top of a flag / selling at the bottom of a flag**: overlap means a range, not a reversal.
- **Entering early on a forming reversal bar**: the close can collapse; wait for the bar to close.
- **Chasing perfect reversal bars across charts**: every reversal is a perfect reversal bar on *some* chart — understand the move, don't hunt patterns.

## Key Takeaways
1. Grade reversal bars: minimum (close beyond open/midpoint), then best-criteria (tails, overlap, follow-through, multi-bar reversal).
2. Reject reversal signals where bars overlap heavily — that's a range.
3. For countertrend: require trend line break + strong reversal bar on the test of the extreme.
4. Watch the final seconds of the bar; a collapsed close turns a reversal signal into a trap.
