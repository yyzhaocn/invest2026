# Chapter 25: Trend Resumption Day

## Core Idea
A trend resumption day has a strong trend in the first hour, then a trading range that lulls traders into thinking the day is over — then the trend resumes in the final hour or two, with the second leg often about the same size as the first.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Recognition**: Strong first-hour trend → hours of sideways action (often a very tight range). Don't give up on the boring midday range — a strong final-hour move is likely. The breakout is usually in the direction of the earlier trend.
- **The 11–12 Noon Trap**: There is frequently a brief 1–2 bar reversal breakout between 11:00 and noon PST that fails, trapping traders into the wrong direction — then the real breakout runs into the close. Use: expect it; the trap is your setup to enter with the morning trend.
- **Scaling-Out-Of-Positions Logic**: During the range, both sides scale in; at some point many reach maximum size. When the breakout comes, the losing side can't scale in anymore — only hope and stop-outs. The trend accelerates as the losing side gives up. Use: wait for the breakout; the collapse/acceleration into the close is often fast.
- **Breakout Pullback Entry**: For traders who didn't enter early or on the breakout, there's often a breakout pullback entry — the pullback to the breakout area.
- **Multi-Day Trend Resumption**: The pattern often spans 2+ days: yesterday's strong spike + trading range can resume today — a simple ABC on the 60-minute chart. Use: be willing to swing a larger position for a big multi-day move.

## Key Concepts
- **Wedge flag / head-and-shoulders flag**: three countertrend lazy pushes in the range; when the third fails to surpass the second, a head-and-shoulders (most fail and become continuation patterns).
- **Tight range = trap warning**: a trap is more likely when the midday range is unusually tight.
- **Weak variant**: a weakly trending countertrend move for a couple of hours that feels like a reversal day but ends up opening on one extreme and closing on the other.

## Mental Models
- Think of the midday range as "position-building": both sides load up; the breakout punishes the loser.
- Think of the noon reversal attempt as a scheduled trap — expect it and fade it with the trend.
- Think of the resumption leg as "the second leg is often about the same size as the first."

## Anti-patterns
- **Giving up on the day during the quiet range**: "the quiet midday sideways movement often leads traders to give up on the day when in fact they should view this as an opportunity."
- **Getting trapped out by the countertrend pushes** (wedge/head-and-shoulders): most head-and-shoulders reversals fail and become continuation patterns.
- **Scaling into the wrong side during the range**: you become the losing side that can't scale in at the breakout.

## Worked Example
(Figure 25.1) A large gap up opens; the market tests the open with a double bottom, then rallies hard to bar 3. From there it trades in a tight range for more than three hours — lulling traders into thinking the good trading is done. Bar 6 reverses up from a poke below a bear trend channel line, dipping one tick below the bar 4 signal bar high — trapping some bears short and many bulls out of their longs. The rally into the close follows (first moving average gap bar of the day as the signal). Additional longs set up off failed breakouts below micro trend lines at bars 7, 9, 10.

## Key Takeaways
1. Strong morning trend + tight midday range = expect trend resumption in the final hours.
2. Fade the failed 11–12 noon reversal breakout with the morning trend.
3. The second leg is often about the same size as the first — swing it.
4. Don't give up on the day; the range is the setup, not the end.
