# Chapter 22: Trending Trading Range Days

## Core Idea
A trending trading range day is a trend made of a series of (usually two) trading ranges separated by brief breakouts. On the daily chart it's clearly a trend day; intraday it looks like ranges stepping in a direction. It's about twice as common as spike-and-channel trend days — and can be traded in both directions.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Recognition Clues**: Opening range ≈ 1/3 to 1/2 of the average daily range; a breakout after an hour or two; then another range forms. The spike is usually only 1–3 bars (smaller, more tails, more overlap) vs. a spike-and-channel day's 3+ large bars. Use: if the day's first-hour range is ~half of average, expect a breakout → measured move → second range.
- **Trading Style by Day Type**: Spike-and-channel day = strong trend → with-trend trades and swings, avoid countertrend. Trending trading range day = two-sided → take trades in both directions, more scalps. Use: classify early; the pullback shape decides — single-bar pullback + spike breakout = spike-and-channel; 5–10 bar sideways pullback = trending trading range.
- **Measured Move & the Second Range**: After the breakout, price often stops near the measured move (height of the first range) and forms a second range. Traders transition from trend style to range style: take profits at the measured move area, expect a test down into the breakout area, then buy low/sell high in the new range.
- **Don't Trail Below Swing Lows**: Once you think it's a trending trading range day, don't trail stops below swing lows — they get hit. Exit on strength at the upper range instead.
- **Fade the Big Bar at the Measured Move**: Aggressive traders fade large trend bars in the measured-move area (exhaustive buy climax) — e.g. short the close/above the high of a big bull bar near the measured move target, expecting a pullback into the breakout gap.
- **Late-Day Reversal Tell**: It's common for the day to reverse through at least one range in the final hour or two — a reliable countertrend trade (breakout test). Most reversal days begin as trending trading range days — always be prepared for a late reversal swing.

## Key Concepts
- **Breakout gap**: the two/three-bar breakout from the lower range; price often comes back to test it.
- **Magnetic pull**: ranges attract breakouts back; most breakout attempts fail.
- **Quiet day alert**: if the range is ~half average into the final hour, expect a breakout (90% of the time the range grows before close).

## Mental Models
- Think of the day as "range → breakout → range" — each range is a comfort zone with a magnet.
- Think of the measured move as the handoff point: trend trading ends, range trading begins.
- Think of the late reversal as a feature: reversal days are born here.

## Anti-patterns
- **Trailing stops below swing lows on these days**: they get hit; exit on strength instead.
- **Trading it like a strong trend day** (with-trend only) when it's two-sided: you miss the range scalps.
- **Assuming a measured move is guaranteed**: in ~1/3 of cases the breakout reverses and the other side extends a little (quiet range day).

The market enters a small trading range for the rest of the day (the second range) — which continues into day 3. The bar 11–19 range is the final bull flag; its breakout to bar 21 fails; a large bear bar (22) breaks the upper range down; the sell-off tests the top of the prior day's range and forms another range. Bar 29: failed breakout of the lower range's top, third push up — the lower range's pull wins; price trades back through the range, tests the bottom at 32, closes near the top.

## Key Takeaways
1. Recognize it: small opening range (1/3–1/2 avg) → breakout → second range.
2. Trade both directions; transition from trend style to range style at the measured move.
3. Don't trail stops below swing lows; exit on strength.
4. Be ready for the late-day reversal — most reversal days start as these.
