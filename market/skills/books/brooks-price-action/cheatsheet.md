# Cheatsheet — Trading Price Action: Trends
*The decisions Brooks would make, on one page. For trend trading.*

## 1. Regime First (before any order)

| See | Verdict | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Trending swings, little body overlap, no climaxes | Strong trend | With-trend only; setups optional |
| Overlapping bars, two-sided | Trading range | Buy low / sell high; fade breakouts |
| Just broke a trend line | Two-sided beginning | Expect a test of the extreme first |
| Spike → pullback → channel | Spike and channel | Spike: trade aggressively; channel: range-style |

**Golden rule**: In a strong trend you don't need a setup — enter at market with a small stop. Setups only minimize risk.

## 2. Decision Rules

- **Always-in read first**: If forced long or short all day, which? Then trade that side.
- **Most breakouts fail** (~most of the time): evaluate reward + risk + probability, not just reward. Don't chase; wait for the pullback or fade the failure.
- **~80% of reversal attempts fail** and become flags. Countertrend only with: trend line break + strong reversal bar on the test of the extreme.
- **Second entries > first entries** (in everything). A *better* fill than the first entry = suspect trap (good fill = bad trade).
- **Wait for the bar to close** before entering. Early entry math never wins (occasionally +1 tick, regularly −8 ticks).
- **Strong trend → weak signal bars are normal.** Perfect-looking pullback setups mean the trend is weak (often the final flag).
- **Don't trail to breakeven in trends** — pullbacks hit it and trap you out; trail below swing lows.
- **If you keep redrawing channel lines, you're on the wrong side** — trade with the trend until it clearly reverses.
- **Losing on every trade = the market flipped; believe the chart, not your hope.**
- **Uncertain? Don't trade.** "There will always be another trade." Losses are hard to overcome.

## 3. Entry Timing by Situation

| Situation | Entry |
|---|---|
| Pullback in bull trend | High 2 with bull signal bar at MA (the classic) |
| Strong spike, missed entry | Buy first pullback after 3+ bar spike; or enter late at market with swing size (Ch 11) |
| 4+ consecutive bull trend bars (non-climactic) | Buy a small position at market |
| Trend just broke a trend line | Don't short the break; buy the failed breakout (breakout pullback) |
| Channel overshoot + reversal bar | Fade it (second penetration best) |
| First reversal attempt in strong move | Skip it; take the second attempt |
| Midday range after strong open trend | Expect noon trap → fade it with the morning trend; resumption into close |
| Small pullback trend, big bars at 11–12 noon | Exhaustive climax → exit longs / consider shorting the close, re-enter with trend after ~10 bars |

## 4. The Strength Checklist (count the signs)

Big gap open · trending swings · most bars trend bars · little body overlap · small/no tails (urgency) · micro gaps · no big climaxes · largest bars often countertrend (trap) · no channel overshoots · sideways corrections after trend line breaks · failed wedges/reversals · 20 MA-gap bars · few profitable countertrend trades · small sideways pullbacks · weak signal bars · repeated two-legged pullbacks · breaks several levels by many ticks.

**≥ ~10 signs** → with-trend only, swing it. **Few signs** → treat as range, trade both ways, scalp.

## 5. Reversal Bar Minimums (grade before countertrend)

Bull: close > open OR close > midpoint. Best: open near/below prior close; lower tail 1/3–1/2; little overlap; strong entry bar follow-through; closes above several bars' closes/highs. Heavy overlap = a range, nothing to reverse — reject. Large doji = one-bar range — wait for second signal.

## 6. Day-Type Classification

| Day type | Clue | Trade it as |
|---|---|---|
| Spike and channel | 3+ big spike bars early, urgent | Strong trend: with-trend + swings |
| Trending trading range | Small opening range, breakout → 2nd range | Both directions, scalps; profits at measured move |
| Trend from the open / small pullback | Extreme in first bars; tiny pullbacks | Strongest: buy weak flags, trail swing lows |
| Trend resumption | Strong open + tight midday range | Fade noon trap, swing the 2nd leg (≈1st leg size) |
| Reversal day | Most start as trending trading range days | On always-in flip: enter quickly, swing most |
| Stairs | 3+ ranges, tested breakouts | Both directions; shrinking stairs = waning momentum |

## 7. Traps & Tells

- **Biggest trend bar of the day is usually countertrend** — trapping beginners. Trade the other direction.
- **Countertrend setups look better than with-trend setups** — that's how trends keep you out.
- **Channel breakout (with-trend) usually fails within ~5 bars** — don't chase it.
- **Noon (11–12 PST) failed reversal breakout** on trend days = scheduled trap; fade it with the trend.
- **A pullback that begins with a strong countertrend spike** may be a reversal channel, not a flag.
- **Good fill = bad trade** on second entries.

## 8. Risk Discipline

- Stops: one tick beyond signal bar / below swing low / trend line area. In strong trends, a small stop at market works.
- Swing trades: ~50% or less probability — take partial profits at first targets.
- Late entries: scale size down as stop widens (dollar risk constant).
- Countertrend: only scalps unless the trend has clearly reversed.
- When in doubt: no trade. The market will offer another one.
