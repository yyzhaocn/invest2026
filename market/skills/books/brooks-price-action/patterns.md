# Patterns — Trading Price Action: Trends

## With-Trend Entry Ladder (Ch 18)
**When to use**: A trend is established (always-in direction clear).
**How**: Work down the ladder as trend strength increases:
1. Buy high 2 pullback at the moving average with a bull signal bar / sell low 2 (bear).
2. Buy wedge bull flag pullback / sell wedge bear flag pullback.
3. Buy breakout pullback after a bull flag breakout / sell after a bear flag.
4. Buy high 1 in a strong bull spike (never after a buy climax) / sell low 1 in a strong bear spike (never after a sell climax).
5. In very strong trends: buy on a stop above a prior swing high / sell below a prior swing low.
**Trade-offs**: Stops confirm direction but cost ticks; higher steps (1–2) are higher-probability; lower steps are earlier but riskier.

## Spike Entry Patterns (Ch 18, Ch 21)
**When to use**: A strong breakout spike is underway (always-in flip).
**How**: Buy the close of every bull trend bar in the spike, at market, or on a limit at/below the low of the prior bar; buy the close of the first bear bar in a bull spike (and mirror for bear). Wide stops required.
**Trade-offs**: Best prices and biggest moves, but requires reading speed and nerve; beginners should use stops, not limit entries in spikes.

## Bar Counting Entries (Intro, Ch 1, Ch 15)
**When to use**: A pullback in a trend or range.
**How**: Count legs — first bar above the prior bar's high = High 1, next = High 2 (ABC), High 3 = wedge. Enter with-trend on the count with a signal bar; the classic best: High 2 with a bull signal bar at the moving average, entry not too close to the top of the channel.
**Trade-offs**: High 1 is earlier (spike pullbacks) but fails more; High 2 is the sweet spot; High 3+ may be wedges (reversal risk if failed).

## Reversal Bar Entry (Ch 5)
**When to use**: End of a pullback or a test of an extreme, with-trend; or countertrend after a trend line break + test.
**How**: Wait for the bar to close; require bull bar close above open/midpoint (bull) with tails 1/3–1/2, little overlap, multi-bar reversal; enter on a stop beyond the bar.
**Trade-offs**: Rejects most overlap-heavy "reversals" (traps); demanding criteria mean fewer trades but higher quality.

## Second Entry (Ch 10)
**When to use**: A first reversal/breakout attempt has failed; strong moves especially.
**How**: Wait for the first attempt to fail, the trend to resume a bar or two, then enter the second attempt. Expect the fill at the same or worse price than the first.
**Trade-offs**: Higher probability; a *better* fill than the first entry is a red flag (good fill = bad trade).

## Late / Missed Entry (Ch 11)
**When to use**: You'd still be holding the swing portion of a trade had you entered at the signal.
**How**: Enter at market with exactly the swing-size position (e.g. 100 of 300 shares) and the same stop you'd have had; add on pauses; trail the whole position's stop.
**Trade-offs**: Logically identical to having held it; emotionally difficult; scale size down as stop widens.

## Trend Channel Line Overshoot Fade (Ch 14, Ch 15)
**When to use**: Price overshoots the channel line, especially the second penetration, with a strong reversal bar.
**How**: Fade the overshoot (short above, buy below) with a reversal bar; or wait for the pullback from a strong trend line break. Treat wedges the same way.
**Trade-offs**: High-probability countertrend scalps; never trade against a trend that keeps redrawing lines — you're on the wrong side.

## Breakout Pullback (Ch 13, Ch 16, Ch 17, Ch 22)
**When to use**: A breakout fails, then its failure fails (the second attempt).
**How**: After a failed breakout, wait for the pullback that tests the breakout area, then enter in the breakout direction with a signal bar. In stairs, size entries/profits from prior breakout distances (ticks).
**Trade-offs**: The second attempt is the real move; requires patience — the first failure is a trap for the other side.

## Micro Trend Line With-Trend Setup (Ch 16)
**When to use**: A strong micro channel exists (2–10 touching bars); always-in clear.
**How**: In a bull micro channel, buy the high of the bar whose low breaks the micro bull trend line (high 1 / failed breakout buy). Mirror for bear. With-trend only.
**Trade-offs**: Great scalps; both-sides-trapped (failed breakout + failed pullback) = best odds; avoid the 1-minute chart — too many signals, too many losers.

## Channel Trading as a Sloping Range (Ch 15)
**When to use**: A broad channel with 5–10 bar swings.
**How**: Buy near the bottom (trend line), sell near the top (channel line) within the channel; at channel start prefer with-trend (buy below lows); near targets switch to countertrend (short above highs). Beginners: with-trend only, best setups only.
**Trade-offs**: The magnetic middle pulls most breakouts back — but a strong with-trend channel breakout can accelerate into a new leg; respect the third-push timing.

## Trending Trading Range Day Trading (Ch 22)
**When to use**: Opening range ~1/3–1/2 of average; breakout → second range.
**How**: Take trades in both directions within each range; take profits at the measured move (height of first range); transition to range style; don't trail stops below swing lows — exit on strength; fade big bars at the measured move target.
**Trade-offs**: Twice as common as spike-and-channel days; late-day reversal is a reliable countertrend trade (most reversal days start here).

## Stairs Trading (Ch 26)
**When to use**: 3+ ranges with trending swings; each breakout tested.
**How**: Trade both directions; swing part of with-trend trades; use prior breakout distances as the tick template for entries/targets (experienced only); watch for shrinking stairs (waning momentum).
**Trade-offs**: Requires range trading skill; beginners should use stop entries only; three pushes ≠ reversal without strong climactic action.

## Trend Resumption (Ch 25)
**When to use**: Strong first-hour trend + midday range.
**How**: Expect the 11–12 noon failed reversal breakout (trap); fade it with the morning trend; enter on the breakout or its pullback; the second leg is often ~the same size as the first — swing it.
**Trade-offs**: The midday lull kills patience; the trap is reliable but fast.
